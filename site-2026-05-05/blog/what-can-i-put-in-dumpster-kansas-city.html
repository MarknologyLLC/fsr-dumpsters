What You Can (and Can't) Throw in a Kansas City Dumpster

Most things you would put on a curb on bulk-pickup day go in our dumpster. Some things do not. This is the complete list, why each rule exists, and what to do with the items we cannot accept.

Published May 5, 2026 · Locally written by Kansas City Dumpster Rentals

The most common Kansas City dumpster rental question we get is some version of "can I throw [X] in there?" The honest answer is: most things, yes. A few specific categories, no, and we explain why.

This is the complete list, organized by category, with what to do for the items we cannot accept.

What You CAN Throw In

Household items

  • Furniture: couches, recliners, dining sets, bedroom sets, office furniture
  • Mattresses and box springs
  • Clothing, shoes, linens, towels, curtains
  • Books, magazines, paper, cardboard
  • Toys, games, sporting equipment
  • Decor, picture frames, artwork (broken)
  • Kitchenware, dishes, glassware
  • Small appliances: toasters, blenders, microwaves, coffee makers

Yard waste

  • Grass clippings, leaves, branches
  • Hedge trimmings, plant debris
  • Soil and root balls (light volumes only; heavy in quantity)
  • Cut firewood

Light construction and renovation debris

  • Drywall (any condition)
  • Wood framing, lumber scraps, plywood, OSB
  • Vinyl flooring, carpet, padding
  • Wood and laminate flooring
  • Cabinets, vanities, countertops (laminate or solid surface)
  • Doors and windows (glass intact)
  • Trim, baseboard, crown molding
  • Insulation
  • Demolition debris (mixed)
  • Light plumbing fixtures (drained sinks, toilets)
  • Light fixtures (no fluorescent bulbs or ballasts)

Non-hazardous appliances

  • Stoves, ovens, range hoods (gas appliances disconnected before disposal)
  • Dishwashers
  • Washing machines, dryers
  • Microwaves, small kitchen appliances

What You CANNOT Throw In

Five categories, with what to do with each.

Category 1: Hazardous Materials

What is on the no-list:

  • Paint (any kind, wet or dry)
  • Solvents and thinners
  • Motor oil, transmission fluid, antifreeze
  • Pesticides and herbicides
  • Cleaning chemicals and household chemicals
  • Pool chemicals
  • Fluorescent light bulbs (the long tube ones)
  • Batteries: car batteries, lithium-ion, alkaline (any kind, in any quantity beyond a few)
  • Propane tanks, butane canisters
  • Aerosol cans (unless completely empty)
  • Asbestos-containing materials

Why: EPA and state regulations classify these as hazardous waste requiring specialty disposal. Putting them in a regular dumpster creates landfill liability and environmental risk.

What to do: Kansas City has a free Household Hazardous Waste program for residents. Drop-offs at scheduled locations or at the central facility (check kcmo.gov for current schedule). Most KC residents can drop off up to 25 gallons or 250 lbs per visit at no charge. For commercial quantities, licensed disposal services exist.

Category 2: Heavy Materials in Volume

What is restricted (small quantities OK, larger volumes not):

  • Concrete
  • Brick, masonry, mortar
  • Dirt, gravel, sand, asphalt
  • Roofing shingles

Why: These materials are extremely heavy. A small amount in a mixed load is fine. A full load of concrete blows past tonnage allowances and the disposal regulations are different. Specialty containers from concrete-specific dumpster providers are the right tool.

What to do for small quantities: A few chunks of concrete or brick in an otherwise normal load is fine.

What to do for large quantities: Order a specialty container from a concrete or roofing dumpster provider in KC. Several local providers handle this. See our roofing dumpster guide for tear-off projects specifically.

Category 3: Refrigerant-Containing Appliances

What we cannot take:

  • Refrigerators
  • Freezers
  • Air conditioners (window units, central AC)
  • Heat pumps
  • Dehumidifiers
  • Wine coolers

Why: Federal law (EPA Section 608) requires the refrigerant (freon, R-410a, etc) to be professionally evacuated before disposal. Dumpster providers are not licensed for this.

What to do: Many Kansas City appliance dealers will pick up old refrigerators when they deliver new ones. Mid-America Regional Council's recycle directory lists local appliance recyclers (often free pickup or small fee). Some KC suburb trash services offer scheduled appliance pickup days.

Category 4: Electronics with Screens

What we cannot take:

  • TVs (any kind, especially CRTs)
  • Computer monitors
  • Tablets, laptops with screens
  • Printers
  • Old phones (any quantity beyond one or two)

Why: KS and MO state e-waste regulations restrict electronics with screens from regular landfills due to lead and mercury content.

What to do: Surplus Exchange (KCK) takes most electronics. Best Buy and Staples have small electronics drop-off (usually free). Goodwill takes working electronics for resale. Some KC suburbs hold annual e-waste drop-off events.

Category 5: Specialty/Regulated Items

What is on the no-list:

  • Tires (any kind)
  • Medical waste, biohazards, sharps (needles)
  • Pharmaceuticals (any quantity)
  • Live ammunition, fireworks (regulated separately)
  • Animal carcasses

Why: Each of these has dedicated disposal channels for safety, environmental, or public health reasons.

What to do:

  • Tires: Most KC tire shops accept old tires for a $2 to $5 per tire fee. Goodyear, Discount Tire, Walmart Auto, and local independents.
  • Medical waste: Pharmacies accept unused pharmaceuticals through take-back programs. Sharps need specialized containers (some pharmacies sell them).
  • Ammunition and fireworks: Local police departments often have buy-back or safe-disposal programs. Do not put in trash, ever.

The Surcharge Policy for Prohibited Items

If our crew finds prohibited items in a dumpster at pickup, surcharges may apply. The amount depends on what was found, how much, and what additional disposal is required. We will document, photograph, and bill on a case-by-case basis. The cleanest path: when in doubt, ask before you load.

The "Maybe" Items (Call Us First)

Some items are not strictly prohibited but worth a quick check before loading:

  • Pianos (heavy, can damage equipment)
  • Hot tubs (oversized, drain first, may require specialty handling)
  • Large quantities of construction debris (call to confirm tonnage)
  • Mattresses (some KC providers exclude them; we accept them)
  • Glass in volume (broken glass in mixed loads is fine; full panes require care)

Loading Rules

Even for accepted items:

  • Do not load above the top edge of the dumpster sidewalls (creates safety and DOT compliance issues during pickup)
  • Heavy items go in first, lighter items on top, distribute weight evenly
  • Load sharp items (broken glass, scrap metal with edges) wrapped or covered
  • Boards, plywood, and 2x4s should be cut into 4 ft pieces or shorter to pack efficiently

The takeaway: Most household and light construction items are welcome. The five categories that need specialty disposal: hazardous chemicals, heavy materials in volume, refrigerant-containing appliances, electronics with screens, and regulated waste (tires, medical, ammunition). When in doubt, call us before you load.

Frequently Asked Questions

What can I put in a Kansas City dumpster?

Most household and light construction debris: furniture, mattresses, household goods, drywall, lumber, vinyl flooring, carpet, yard waste, light demolition, non-freon appliances. Anything you would normally bag for bulk-pickup, with hazardous, heavy, and regulated material exceptions.

What cannot go in a dumpster?

Hazardous materials (paint, chemicals, batteries, tires, asbestos), refrigerant appliances, electronics with screens, medical waste, and heavy materials in volume (concrete, brick, dirt, roofing shingles). Each has a dedicated KC disposal channel.

Can I throw a couch in a Kansas City dumpster?

Yes. Furniture is welcome. Mattresses count toward tonnage allowance, so size up if you have multiple.

If your project has an item not covered here, just ask. Request a quote with a list of what you are throwing and we will tell you upfront what fits and what needs alternative disposal.
